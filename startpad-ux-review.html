00 — Method & honesty note

What this review is based on

The live site could not be loaded from this environment — startpad.me is blocked by the network egress policy on this machine, so I could not crawl the real screens, click the real flows, or measure the real performance. Everything below is built from the product's public positioning: a gamified launchpad that guides MENA founders from idea to launch in 15 missions, with AI tools, mentors and a community.

So every finding is tagged. verify means I am asserting something about your current build that you should confirm against the live screen before acting — it is a hypothesis with a fix attached. build means the recommendation is net-new and holds regardless of what exists today.

If you send me screenshots of the landing page, the signup flow, the dashboard and one mission screen — or unblock the domain — I will re-run this against the real pixels and replace every verify with a confirmed observation, in about an hour of work.

Heuristics applied: Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics, the Hooked model (trigger → action → reward → investment) for the mission loop, Duolingo/Rise-style progression mechanics as the gamification benchmark, WCAG 2.2 AA for accessibility, and mobile-first + Arabic RTL as the two non-negotiables for this specific audience.

01 — Audience thesis

Eight things that are true about your user that are not true about a 35-year-old founder

Every recommendation in this document descends from one of these. If a design decision doesn't serve one of them, it is decoration.

1. The phone is the product

MENA Gen Z is one of the most mobile-dominant cohorts on earth — assume 75–85% of your sessions are a phone held in one hand, often on cellular data, often at night. A "mission" that is really a desktop-shaped form with eight text fields does not exist for this user. Missions must be completable on a phone, in portrait, with a thumb, in under 10 minutes.

2. They will judge you in 5 seconds, and they judge by look

This cohort grew up on products with world-class design as the baseline. Visual quality is read as a proxy for legitimacy. A stock-photo hero, a Bootstrap-blue button, or a lorem-ish testimonial doesn't read as "early stage" to them — it reads as "not real". The fastest trust win available to you is a genuinely well-crafted landing page.

3. Signup is a tax they resent paying before seeing value

Asking for an account before they have felt anything is the single biggest conversion leak in products like this. Let them do something first — a real mission, an AI idea-score, a validation checklist — and put the account wall at the moment they want to save the result. Reverse the order and top-of-funnel conversion typically moves in double digits.

4. Progress is the reward, but only if it maps to something real

Streaks, XP and badges work — this cohort is fluent in them. But they are also cynical about hollow points. Tie every reward to an artifact they can show someone: a validated problem statement, a landing page, ten customer interviews, a pitch deck. The badge is a receipt, not a sticker.

5. Peer proof beats authority proof

A wall of accelerator logos does less than one 22-year-old from Cairo saying "I got my first 10 users through mission 7". Faces, ages, cities, real numbers, real screenshots. Your users are your best marketing asset and most platforms in this category never surface them.

6. If it isn't shareable, it doesn't spread

Growth for a Gen Z product is a screenshot in a WhatsApp group or an Instagram story. Every milestone should generate an image worth posting: a founder card, a mission-complete card, a launch card with the startup's name on it. Design the share asset with as much care as the app screen.

7. Arabic is not a toggle, it is a first-class build

For MENA Gen Z, the natural register is Arabic — often mixed with English. A right-to-left layout is not a mirrored stylesheet: it is icon direction, progress direction, number formatting, form alignment, Arabic-native type sizing and line-height, and copy written by a human in the tone this cohort actually speaks. Half-done RTL reads worse than English-only.

8. They are the most fraud-literate generation, so be radically clear

No hidden pricing, no fake countdown timers, no "free" that becomes a card form on step three, no fake scarcity. State prices in local currency, disclose what the AI does with their idea, and make cancellation one tap. Clarity is a competitive advantage here, not a compliance chore.

02 — Journey audit

The eleven journeys, in the order a founder lives them

Numbering here is a real sequence: J1 is the first second of contact, J10 is the day they come back, J11 runs across all of them. Severity is P0 ship-blocking, P1 this quarter, P2 when there's room.

J1 · Landing & first impression

Goal: a stranger arriving from Instagram, TikTok or a friend's link understands what this is and starts a mission — without reading a paragraph.

UX-01 P0 verify

The hero explains the product instead of showing it

"Launch your startup in 15 guided missions" is a good line, but a headline plus a button is a brochure. Fix: put a live, clickable artifact above the fold — an idea input that returns a real AI validation score in ~3 seconds, or an interactive mission map you can drag. The hero's job is to make them do something, not read something.

UX-02 P0 verify

The 15 missions are the product and are probably invisible

If a visitor can't see what the 15 missions actually are before signing up, they're being asked to buy a sealed box. Fix: a public, crawlable mission map — each mission with its name, outcome, time estimate and the artifact it produces. This doubles as your strongest SEO surface.

UX-03 P1 build

No proof from people the visitor recognizes as themselves

Fix: a "founders on Startpad right now" strip — faces, ages, cities, what they're building, which mission they're on. Live counters ("1,284 missions completed this week") beat static testimonials for this cohort.

UX-04 P1 verify

One CTA for two very different visitors

A student with no idea and a founder with a half-built product need different doors. Fix: a two-path hero — "I have an idea" / "I don't have an idea yet" — which routes to different first missions. The second path is where most of this audience actually sits and almost nobody serves it.

UX-05 P1 build

Mobile hero density

Fix: on phones, the fold should carry headline, one-line subhead, the interactive element, and a sticky bottom CTA bar — nothing else. Everything currently competing for that space moves below.

J2 · Signup & account creation

Goal: get from interested to identified with the least possible friction, at the right moment.

UX-06 P0 verify

The wall is in the wrong place

Fix: anonymous-first. Let them complete mission zero in a local session, then trigger the account prompt with "Save your progress" — the ask now has an obvious payoff. Migrate the anonymous session into the new account on signup; losing their work at this step is fatal.

UX-07 P0 build

Auth methods must match the region

Fix: Google and Apple one-tap, plus phone number OTP — phone-first is the norm across MENA and a password field is a drop-off. Password-based email signup should be the last option shown, not the first.

UX-08 P1 verify

Everything is asked at once

Fix: progressive profiling. Signup collects one identifier. Country, stage, industry, university and goals get collected inside the experience, one question at a time, each attached to a reason ("so we can match you to mentors in your country").

UX-09 P2 build

Error states in auth are where trust dies

Fix: every auth error names the problem and the next step — "That code expired. Send a new one?" with the button right there. Never "Invalid request".

J3 · Onboarding & first value

Goal: from account created to first real artifact produced, in one sitting. This is the number that decides whether the company works.

UX-10 P0 build

Define and defend a 10-minute time-to-first-artifact

Fix: onboarding ends when the founder is holding something — an AI-scored idea one-pager, a named problem statement, a customer-interview script. Not when a profile is filled in. Everything that doesn't serve that 10 minutes gets deferred.

UX-11 P1 build

No path for "I don't have an idea"

Fix: a guided idea-discovery mission — interests, skills, problems they personally face, communities they belong to → AI generates 5 candidate directions grounded in MENA market context. This is the highest-leverage missing surface in the whole product for a student audience.

UX-12 P1 verify

Onboarding as a wall of text

Fix: one question per screen, big tap targets, visible step count, a back button that actually works, and an escape hatch ("skip for now") on everything non-essential. Never a modal tour with tooltips on a page they haven't earned yet.

J4 · The mission loop — your core mechanic

Goal: a founder opens the app, knows exactly what to do next, does it, and feels measurably closer to launch.

UX-13 P0 build

The dashboard must answer one question: what do I do right now?

Fix: the home screen leads with a single "next action" card — mission name, the concrete task, minutes required, one button. Progress ring, streak and stats sit beneath it. Grids of equal-weight cards force a decision the user cannot make.

UX-14 P0 build

Missions need to be broken into steps that fit a bus ride

Fix: every mission = 3–6 steps, each ≤5 minutes, each individually completable and saved. A "mission" as one long page is a mission that gets abandoned at 40%. Auto-save every field; never lose input to a refresh.

UX-15 P0 build

Completion must feel like something

Fix: a real completion moment — the artifact they produced, the XP earned, what unlocks next, and a share card generated on the spot. A toast that says "Saved" wastes the single most motivating instant in the product.

UX-16 P1 build

Show the shape of the journey, always

Fix: a persistent mission map — a path with 15 nodes: done, current, locked. Founders need to see the distance travelled and the distance left. This screen is also your best shareable ("I'm on mission 9 of 15").

UX-17 P1 build

Locking should tease, not block blankly

Fix: a locked mission still shows its name, its outcome, and exactly what unlocks it. A grey padlock with no information kills anticipation instead of creating it.

UX-18 P2 build

Nothing rescues a stalled founder

Fix: if a mission sits untouched for 72 hours, offer a smaller version of it ("just do step 1, 3 minutes"), a worked example from another founder, or a nudge into the community. Stall-recovery is where retention is won.

J5 · AI tools

Goal: AI that does the founder's homework with them, not a chat box bolted to the side.

UX-19 P0 build

Contextual AI beats a generic chatbot every time

Fix: the AI lives inside the mission step, already knowing the founder's idea, country and stage, with 3 suggested actions specific to that step ("sharpen this problem statement", "write 5 interview questions for this segment"). An empty chat input asks the user to do the hard part.

UX-20 P1 build

Output must be editable and ownable

Fix: every AI output lands in an editable field with regenerate, shorten, translate to Arabic, and "use this" actions. Founders must feel authorship — an uneditable answer feels like homework someone else did.

UX-21 P1 build

Streaming, or it feels broken

Fix: stream tokens, show a skeleton with a real status line ("reading your idea…", "checking the market…"), and never a bare spinner over 2 seconds. Also state what the AI can't do, so a wrong answer doesn't break trust.

UX-22 P2 build

Disclose the data deal plainly

Fix: one plain-language line where the AI is used: what's sent, whether it trains anything, who can see the idea. This audience checks.

J6 · Mentors

Goal: booking a human should feel as light as sending a DM, and as safe as a scheduled class.

UX-23 P0 build

Mentor browsing needs the filters this audience actually thinks in

Fix: filter by country, language (Arabic / English / both), expertise, stage they help with, and availability this week. Show response time and how many founders they've helped. A grid of headshots with job titles is a directory, not a matching tool.

UX-24 P0 build

Booking friction and status ambiguity

Fix: pick a slot in the founder's own timezone, state the call length and language, confirm on-screen and by WhatsApp/email, add-to-calendar, and a visible state machine — requested / confirmed / completed / no-show. Ambiguity after booking is the top complaint in every mentorship product.

UX-25 P1 build

No prep, no value

Fix: auto-generate a one-page brief from the founder's mission progress and send it to the mentor before the call; give the founder 3 suggested questions. This single feature roughly doubles perceived mentor quality with no extra mentor effort.

UX-26 P2 build

Nothing happens after the call

Fix: a post-call screen — rate, capture 3 action items, and turn them into tasks inside the current mission. Close the loop or the call evaporates.

J7 · Community

Goal: never feel like the only person doing this. The #1 reason solo founders quit is isolation.

UX-27 P0 verify

A link out to Discord is not a community feature

If community means "join our server", you lose the context that makes it valuable. Fix: an in-product feed scoped to mission and stage, so a founder on mission 4 sees what other founders on mission 4 are posting. Keep Discord for chat; keep the structured, contextual layer in-app.

UX-28 P1 build

Empty-first posting is intimidating

Fix: prompt-based posting — "share your mission 3 artifact for feedback", "ask one question about pricing". Structured prompts get 5–10× the participation of a blank composer, especially from people posting in a second language.

UX-29 P1 build

Cohorts create accountability that feeds do not

Fix: auto-group founders who started the same month into a small circle (8–15 people) with a shared progress board. Small groups outperform a global feed for this audience by a wide margin.

UX-30 P2 build

Safety and moderation are a design surface

Fix: report and block on every post, visible community rules, and rate limits for new accounts. For a young audience this is table stakes, and it needs to exist before you scale, not after.

J8 · Identity, proof & sharing

Goal: the founder gets something to show for the work — publicly.

UX-31 P0 build

There is no public founder profile

Fix: startpad.me/@username — their startup, stage, missions completed, badges, artifacts they chose to publish, and a "back me" or "give feedback" button. This is simultaneously a status object, a growth loop and an SEO surface. It is the highest-ROI page you can add.

UX-32 P0 build

Milestones generate nothing shareable

Fix: auto-generated share cards sized for Instagram Stories (1080×1920) and X/LinkedIn (1200×630), with the founder's name, startup, mission and progress. One tap to share to WhatsApp, Instagram, X, LinkedIn. This is your cheapest acquisition channel by an order of magnitude.

UX-33 P1 build

Launched startups aren't celebrated publicly

Fix: a Launch Wall — every startup that finished all 15 missions, with the founder, country, and a link. Aspiration for new users, reward for finishers, and a page journalists and investors will actually browse.

J9 · Pricing & monetization

Goal: convert without a single dark pattern, from an audience with genuinely low disposable income.

UX-34 P0 verify

Pricing must be visible before signup

Fix: a public pricing page with prices in local currency (EGP, SAR, AED), what's free forever, what's paid, and no card required for free. Hidden pricing reads as a trap to this cohort and they leave rather than ask.

UX-35 P1 build

No student tier for a student audience

Fix: a verified student price (university email), plus regional purchasing-power pricing. Charging Cairo the same as Dubai loses Cairo entirely.

UX-36 P1 build

The paywall should arrive at a moment of momentum

Fix: trigger the upgrade screen right after a completed mission, framed against what they've already built — "you've validated the problem; unlock mentor calls to pressure-test it". Show progress preserved, list exactly what unlocks, offer monthly and one-time. Never interrupt mid-mission.

UX-37 P2 build

Local payment methods

Fix: cards plus the regional rails this audience actually holds — Apple Pay, Fawry/Vodafone Cash in Egypt, mada in Saudi. A card-only checkout excludes a large share of your target users outright.

J10 · Coming back

Goal: a founder who left on Tuesday returns on Thursday.

UX-38 P0 build

No re-entry trigger

Fix: web push + email lifecycle keyed to behaviour, not calendar: mission incomplete at 24h, streak about to break, a mentor replied, someone commented on your artifact, your cohort passed you. One channel, low volume, always naming the next concrete action.

UX-39 P1 build

Install it on the home screen

Fix: ship a PWA — installable, offline shell, push notifications. For MENA mobile users this is meaningfully better than pushing an app-store download, and far cheaper than native.

J11 · Cross-cutting — the layer that touches every screen

UX-40 P0 build

Arabic & RTL as a first-class build

Fix: full dir="rtl" support with logical CSS properties throughout, a proper Arabic typeface at its own size and line-height scale (Arabic needs more leading than Latin), mirrored icons and progress direction, Arabic numerals handled per locale convention, and copy written natively — not translated. Language choice offered on first run, persisted, and reflected in the URL for SEO.

UX-41 P1 build

Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA

Fix: 4.5:1 text contrast (audit every muted grey and every colour-on-accent button), 44×44px minimum tap targets, visible focus rings, real labels on every input, keyboard-operable modals with focus trapping, prefers-reduced-motion respected on all the gamification animation, and status changes announced to screen readers.

UX-42 P1 build

Empty, loading, error and offline states are unowned

Fix: design all four for every list and every async surface. Empty states get an illustration, one line of encouragement and one action. Loading uses skeletons shaped like the content. Errors say what happened and how to retry. Offline says "you're offline — your work is saved" rather than failing silently. On 3G, target LCP under 2.5s and defer everything non-critical.

04 — Design system

What the interface should look like for this audience

Colour

One high-energy accent, used with discipline, on a quiet ground. The failure mode for Gen Z products is six bright colours competing — it reads as a children's app, not a serious tool. Keep semantic colours (success, warning, danger) separate from the accent, and pass 4.5:1 on every text pairing, in both themes. Dark mode is not optional for this cohort; a large share of them use their phone in dark mode by default, at night.

Type

A confident sans with real weight contrast — 700/800 for headlines set tight, 400 for body at 16–17px minimum on mobile. Pair it with a monospace face for progress numbers, XP, mission codes and timers: it makes the gamification read as instrumentation rather than decoration. For Arabic, choose an Arabic-native family (IBM Plex Sans Arabic, Noto Sans Arabic, Cairo) and give it its own scale — Arabic generally needs ~1.1× the size and ~1.2× the line-height of the Latin equivalent to feel right.

Motion

Reserve animation for three moments: mission completion, XP/progress increments, and unlocks. Everything else should be near-instant. 200–300ms, spring-eased, and completely disabled under prefers-reduced-motion. Ambient animation on a productivity surface is noise, and it costs battery on the mid-range Androids much of this audience is using.

Components worth building properly

Next-action card

The single most important component in the product. Mission, task, time, one button.

Mission node

Four states — locked, available, in-progress, complete — legible without colour alone.

Progress ring + streak

Tabular numerals, animated increment, meaningful at a glance.

AI panel

Docked, context-aware, streaming, with editable output and suggested actions.

Share card generator

Canvas-rendered, two aspect ratios, one-tap to WhatsApp and Stories.

Bottom nav + sheets

Thumb-reachable, 5 items max, bottom sheets instead of centre modals on mobile.

Voice

Direct, warm, peer-level. Never corporate, never try-hard slang — this audience detects a brand imitating them instantly and it's worse than being plain.

07 — Lovable prompts

Twenty-four prompts, in build order

P00 · Master context — run this first
You are building Startpad — a gamified launchpad that guides Gen Z founders in the MENA region from idea to launch through 15 missions, with AI tools, mentors and a community.

Set up the foundations before building any page:

DESIGN SYSTEM
- Mobile-first. Every screen is designed for a 390px viewport first, then scaled up. Bottom sheets instead of centered modals on mobile. Bottom tab nav with max 5 items, thumb-reachable.
- Colors: one high-energy accent used sparingly on a quiet neutral ground. Separate semantic colors for success / warning / danger. Full dark mode via CSS custom properties on :root — dark mode is a first-class theme, not an afterthought.
- Type: a confident sans with strong weight contrast (700/800 headlines set tight, 400 body at 16-17px minimum on mobile). A monospace face for XP, timers, mission codes and progress numbers, with tabular numerals.
- Motion: only for mission completion, XP increments, and unlocks. 200-300ms, spring easing. Everything else instant. Fully disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.
- Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA. 4.5:1 text contrast, 44x44px minimum tap targets, visible focus rings, real labels on every input, keyboard-operable dialogs with focus trapping.
- Every list and async surface needs four designed states: empty, loading (skeletons shaped like the content), error (says what happened + how to retry), and offline.

VOICE
Direct, warm, peer-level. Never corporate, never imitation slang. Buttons say exactly what happens. Errors name the problem and the fix.

TECH
React + TypeScript + Tailwind + shadcn/ui. Supabase for auth and data. All colors and spacing as design tokens — no hardcoded hex values in components. Build the token file and the base component set (Button, Card, Sheet, Input, Badge, Progress, Skeleton, Toast) before any page.

Confirm the token file and base components, then stop. I'll send pages one at a time.
P01 · Landing page rebuild
Rebuild the Startpad landing page (/) to convert a 21-year-old founder arriving from Instagram or a friend's WhatsApp link.

ABOVE THE FOLD (mobile first)
- Headline: "Launch your startup in 15 missions." Subhead in one line, no paragraph.
- An INTERACTIVE element, not a static image: an input where a visitor types their startup idea in one sentence and gets a live AI validation score in ~3 seconds — score out of 100, one strength, one risk, one suggested first mission. It must work with NO account.
- Two-path CTA: "I have an idea" and "I don't have an idea yet" — these route to different first missions.
- Sticky bottom CTA bar on mobile that appears after the hero scrolls out.

BELOW THE FOLD, in this order
1. The 15 missions as a visual path — names, outcomes, time estimates. Make it scrollable horizontally on mobile and clickable to /missions.
2. Peer proof strip: real founder faces with age, city, what they're building, which mission they're on. Live counter of missions completed this week.
3. What you walk away with: the concrete artifacts (validated problem, landing page, pitch deck, first 10 users), shown as objects, not bullet points.
4. Mentors: faces, countries, languages they speak.
5. Pricing summary with a link to /pricing. State clearly what's free forever.
6. FAQ answering: is it free, is it in Arabic, do I need an idea, how long does it take, who owns my idea.

Language switcher (English / العربية) in the header, persisted. LCP under 2.5s on 3G — no heavy hero video, lazy-load everything below the fold.
P02 · /try — no-account mission zero
Create /try — a complete first mission that a visitor can finish WITHOUT creating an account. This is the most important conversion surface in the product.

FLOW (one question per screen, 4 steps, ~5 minutes total)
1. "What problem are you trying to solve?" — free text, with 3 example answers shown as tappable chips to reduce blank-page paralysis.
2. "Who has this problem?" — free text + quick-select chips (students, small business owners, parents, freelancers, gamers).
3. "How do they solve it today?" — free text.
4. AI generates a one-page "Idea Snapshot": sharpened problem statement, target segment, 3 riskiest assumptions, a validation score out of 100, and the 3 next missions to run.

RULES
- Progress saved to localStorage after every step. Refreshing must never lose input.
- Visible step indicator (1 of 4) and a working back button.
- Stream the AI output with a real status line ("reading your idea…", "checking assumptions…"), never a bare spinner.
- The account wall appears ONLY at the end, framed as "Save your Idea Snapshot" — with the snapshot visible and blurred-but-readable behind it. On signup, migrate the localStorage session into the new account so nothing is lost.
- Let them download the snapshot as an image and share it to WhatsApp / Instagram Stories without an account.

Works fully in Arabic RTL.
P03 · Auth & account creation
Rebuild signup and login for a MENA mobile audience.

METHOD ORDER (top to bottom)
1. Continue with Google
2. Continue with Apple
3. Continue with phone number — OTP via SMS, with country picker defaulting to the user's detected country (EG, SA, AE, JO, KW first in the list)
4. Email + password, collapsed under "More options"

RULES
- Signup asks for ONE identifier. No name, no country, no company, no "how did you hear about us" at this step.
- If an anonymous /try session exists in localStorage, migrate it into the new account immediately after auth and land the user on their saved Idea Snapshot — never on an empty dashboard.
- OTP input: 6 separate boxes, auto-advance, paste-from-SMS support, auto-submit on the last digit, a visible countdown before "Resend code" becomes tappable.
- Every error names the problem and the next step: "That code expired. Send a new one?" with the button inline. Never "Invalid request".
- Loading states on every button, disabled while pending, no double-submit.
- Login and signup are the same screen — detect whether the identifier exists and adapt.

Full Arabic RTL, including the phone number field (number stays LTR inside an RTL layout).
P04 · Onboarding wizard
Build /onboarding — it must end with the founder holding a real artifact within 10 minutes of signup, not with a filled-in profile.

STRUCTURE — one question per screen, large tap targets, step counter, working back button, "skip for now" on everything non-essential:
1. "What are you building?" — or "I'm still figuring it out", which routes to the idea-discovery path below.
2. Stage — visual cards: just an idea / talking to users / building it / already launched.
3. Country — flags, MENA countries first.
4. What you want from Startpad — multi-select: validate my idea, find co-founder, get first users, raise money, learn.
5. → Straight into their first mission. NOT a dashboard.

IDEA DISCOVERY PATH (for "still figuring it out")
Ask: what are you good at, what communities are you part of, what annoys you weekly, what have you built before. Then AI generates 5 candidate startup directions grounded in MENA market context, each with a one-line why-now. They pick one and it becomes their idea.

Each question states why it's being asked ("so we can match you to mentors in your country"). Answers save immediately — closing the tab mid-flow and returning resumes at the same step.

P16 · Arabic & RTL
