Three rules that apply to every question.

  1. Every list ends with "Other →", which opens a text field. No list is ever closed.
  2. Every question can take an attachment, whatever its type. Answering a chip question and wanting to attach a screenshot is normal.
  3. Multi-select is the default for categories. Fintech and healthtech is a real answer. Subscription and commission is a real answer.

Where cards get used. card-match is not decoration — it does two jobs no other input does:

  • It teaches. Matching the nine Business Model Canvas blocks to their plain-English meanings takes sixty seconds and replaces a paragraph nobody reads.
  • It forces justification. Matching each MVP feature to the problem it solves means a feature with nothing to pair to is scope creep, visibly, without anyone saying so.

Finance isn't a mission and it shouldn't be a block of questions bolted onto one. It's one thread running through seven missions, where each step uses numbers the founder already gave you.

Seventeen money questions in total. Nine of them are calculated — the founder never types them. They enter a price and a cost in Mission 8 and the platform returns their margin and their break-even. They enter a budget and a result in Mission 11 and get a cost per customer. By Mission 15 the runway calculates itself.

Four rules, so this doesn't become the thing that stops people.

  1. "Not sure yet", "Nothing", and "No revenue yet" are always available and never penalised. Most founders at Mission 14 are pre-revenue. That's normal, and a rubric that marks it down teaches people to invent numbers.
  2. Nothing financial is asked in Discover, Shape or Test. Missions 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 9 and 10 have no money questions at all. A founder who hasn't defined the problem yet has nothing useful to say about price.
  3. Estimate or actual, on every number. A toggle, stored with the value. An estimated runway and a real one are different things and the founder should be able to say which they meant.
  4. Currency is set once, on the profile — EGP · SAR · AED · USD — and every field inherits it. Never ask twice.

Connect these to the toolkit. The platform already has unit-economics, burn-and-runway and P&L calculators. Mission 8's numbers should open in the unit-economics calculator, and Mission 15's should open in burn and runway, pre-filled — one place to enter, two places to use.

PROMPT A — Build the Five Missing Interaction Types

PROMPT A · Build the Five Missing Interaction Types
PAGE: The shared question component library.

THE PROBLEM

The missions need drag-based interactions that don't exist in the codebase
yet. Everything is currently a text field, which is why the missions feel
like paperwork.

BUILD THESE FIVE. They are the ones that change how the journey feels.

1. CARD-MATCH — drag cards to pair them up
   Two columns of cards. The founder drags a card from the left onto its
   partner on the right, or taps one then the other.
   - A joined pair shows a connecting line and both cards lock together
   - Unmatched cards stay visibly unmatched — that IS the feedback
   - Some exercises have a correct answer (matching canvas blocks to their
     meanings): show a gentle tick on a correct pair, and let a wrong pair be
     dragged apart with no penalty and no score
   - Some have no correct answer (matching features to benefits): every pair
     is the founder's own judgement, nothing is marked
   - Allow one-to-many where the question says so
   - MOBILE: tap-to-select then tap-to-pair. Dragging across two columns on a
     phone is miserable.
   - KEYBOARD: arrow keys to move between cards, space to pick up and drop
   Used 7 times, including matching the nine Business Model Canvas blocks to
   their plain-English meanings, and matching each MVP feature to the problem
   it solves.

2. CARD-SORT — drag cards into labelled columns
   Two or three columns with headings. Cards start in an unsorted tray and are
   dragged into a column.
   - The tray empties as they sort; an empty tray means done
   - Column counts shown in the heading: "In the MVP (4)"
   - MOBILE: each card gets buttons for the columns instead of dragging
   Used 6 times. The most important is Mission 13: every feature sorted into
   "In the MVP" or "Not yet".

3. RANK — drag items into order
   A vertical list, dragged to reorder, position number always visible.
   - Pre-order it sensibly where earlier data allows, so the founder is
     adjusting rather than starting from nothing
   - MOBILE: up/down arrows on each row as well as dragging
   Used 4 times, including ranking 13 go-to-market channels.

4. CHECKLIST — tick all that apply
   Grouped checkboxes with optional group headings. Used both for gathering
   facts and for self-assessment, where the boxes teach the criteria by making
   the founder apply them.

5. DROPDOWN — long searchable list
   For lists too long for chips. Type to filter, multi-select where the
   question allows it, selected items shown as removable chips beneath.

RULES FOR ALL FIVE, AND FOR EVERY EXISTING TYPE

  a. EVERY LIST ENDS WITH "Other →" which opens a text field, and what the
     founder types there is stored so you can see what your taxonomies are
     missing.
  b. EVERY QUESTION CAN CARRY AN ATTACHMENT whatever its type.
  c. MULTI-SELECT IS THE DEFAULT for categories. Fintech AND healthtech is a
     real answer. Subscription AND commission is a real answer.
  d. FULL KEYBOARD ACCESS AND SCREEN-READER LABELS on every drag interaction.
     A drag-only control that cannot be operated another way is broken.
  e. RTL FOR ARABIC — columns mirror, drag direction mirrors, rank order
     mirrors.
  f. WORKS AT 390px WIDE. This is where missions get answered.
  g. RESPECT prefers-reduced-motion: no card animation for those users.

THE PROBLEM

87 questions across the 15 missions, 75 of them blank text areas. Testers
could not answer basic questions — "what area are you interested in?" — not
because the question is hard, but because a blank box asks the founder to
invent both the answer and the shape of the answer at once.

THE FIX

Replace the question set with the 120 questions in the accompanying document.
Load them as DATA, never as JSX. Each question is a record:

  { id, missionId, phase, order, type, label, helpText,
    options[], allowOther, required, carriesFrom,
    attachmentAllowed, exampleAnswerId, config }

  config holds the type-specific parts:
    card-match   { leftSource, rightSource, hasCorrectAnswer, allowOneToMany }
    card-sort    { columns[], cardSource }
    rank         { items[], preOrderBy }
    matrix       { xAxis, yAxis, quadrantLabels[], cardSource }
    sentence-builder { template, blanks[] }
    number       { units[], currencies[], calculated }

THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE: STRUCTURE THE SCAFFOLDING, NEVER THE CONTENT.

A list of industries does not tell a founder what their business is — it
removes the burden of inventing a taxonomy from scratch. The thinking stays
theirs. Never pre-fill an answer, never suggest what they should conclude,
never narrow the options so far that only one is choosable.

THREE QUESTIONS THAT MUST BE BUILT EXACTLY AS WRITTEN

  MISSION 1 Q7 — "How do you know this is real?" includes the option
  "It's a hunch for now". That option must be selectable with NO penalty and
  no warning. It is the honest answer for most founders in Mission 1, and
  punishing it teaches people to lie to the platform.

  MISSION 7 Q8 — the verdict includes "Inconclusive", and the helper text says
  it is a respected answer. Without that, founders invent a verdict.

  MISSION 14 — there is NO product-market-fit score anywhere on this mission.
  Wherever the platform currently shows one, replace it with EVIDENCE
  STRENGTH — thin / early / promising — computed from sample size and
  observation window, never from self-rating.

CARRY THIRTEEN FIELDS FORWARD, pre-filled and editable, framed as
continuation rather than repetition:

  M2 Q3   <- M1 Q2                   the problem
  M3 cards<- M1, M2                  problems to prioritise
  M4 Q2   <- M2 Q1                   the user
  M6      <- M2, M4, M1 Q4           segment, problem, alternatives
  M7 Q1   <- M6 "Must be true" pile  riskiest assumption
  M8      <- M2, M6                  segments, value proposition
  M9 Q1   <- M6, M7                  untested assumptions
  M9 Q4   <- M6                      features
  M10 Q7  <- M9 Q1                   did the prototype answer its question
  M11 Q3  <- M2                      narrowed segment
  M13 Q1  <- M6, M9                  features to scope
  M13 Q2  <- M3                      problems to match features against
  M15 Q2  <- M3,4,7,10,11,12,14      the evidence pack

  Never show a carried field blank. Never show it locked. When a founder edits
  one, keep both versions — at the end of the journey they can see how their
  problem statement changed from Mission 1 to Mission 15.

ARABIC: every question label, every chip, every column heading and every card
exists in both languages, written natively rather than translated.

PROMPT C — Make It Feel Like Something, Not a Form

PROMPT C · Make It Feel Like Something, Not a Form
PAGE: /missions/:id — the answering experience.

THE PROBLEM

Even with better inputs, a page of stacked questions is still a form. The
difference between a form and something a founder wants to finish is almost
entirely in the moment-to-moment feel.

THE FIX

1. ONE QUESTION PER SCREEN on mobile, two or three on desktop. A stepper shows
   "Question 3 of 7" and allows jumping. Give each question room.

2. DRAG EXERCISES GET THE WHOLE SCREEN. A card-sort or a matrix is the mission
   at that moment — nothing else competes with it. No sidebar, no banner, no
   progress card.

3. CARDS BEHAVE LIKE OBJECTS. Lift on pick-up, a shadow while moving, settle
   into place, a drop zone that highlights when a card is over it. This is
   where the feeling of a product rather than a questionnaire comes from, and
   it is about fifteen lines of CSS.

4. NAME THE RESULT BACK. When a card lands somewhere meaningful, say what it
   means in one line:
     "Fix first — this one is both painful and constant."
     "Nothing matched to this feature. Is it needed in the MVP?"
   One line. That line is the teaching.

5. "SEE AN EXAMPLE" ON EVERY QUESTION — a real answer that scored well, shown
   in a panel, NEVER pre-filled into the field, and always from a different
   industry than the founder's so it cannot be copied.

6. QUIET FEEDBACK. A small tick and "Ready" when an answer meets the bar. Not
   a score, not a celebration — a signal they can move on.

7. MOMENTUM, NOT CONFETTI. A small progress movement between questions,
   something bigger at mission completion. Do not celebrate trivial actions —
   this audience finds that patronising faster than most.

8. SAVE VISIBLY. "Saved 2 minutes ago" near the stepper. Returning restores
   the exact position, including a half-finished card sort.

9. SHOW THE THREAD between missions:
     "In Mission 2 you defined your user as [X]. This mission turns that into
      a problem statement you can test."

10. LET THEM SEE THE WHOLE MISSION. A "view all questions" toggle, for the
    people who need the map before the first step.

11. TIME PER QUESTION, honestly: "about 2 minutes". A card sort that says
    "about 1 minute" gets started; an unlabelled one gets postponed.

12. ARABIC AND RTL throughout — stepper direction, chip wrapping, drag
    direction, column order, matrix axes.

PROMPT D — The Money Thread

PROMPT D · The Money Thread
PAGE: /missions/4, 5, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 — and a shared finance layer.

IMPORTANT: Missions 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 9 and 10 get NO money questions at all. A
founder who has not yet defined the problem has nothing useful to say about
price, and asking makes the early missions feel like an accounting exam.

THE PROBLEM

Finance currently appears as a few scattered number fields with no relationship
to each other. A founder enters a price in one mission and a budget in another
and the platform never connects them — so nobody ever learns their margin,
their break-even, what a customer costs, or how long their money lasts. These
are the numbers every incubator asks for and the ones founders are most afraid
of.

THE FIX — ONE THREAD ACROSS SEVEN MISSIONS. Each step uses numbers already
entered. NINE OF THE SEVENTEEN ARE CALCULATED — the founder never types them.

  M4   already asks what the problem costs the user. Keep it. Tag it as the
       first node in the money thread so it appears in the summary.

  M5   NEW, single-select band: "Roughly what would it cost to build?"
         Nothing but my own time · Under 5,000 · 5,000-25,000 ·
         25,000-100,000 · Over 100,000 · NOT SURE YET
       A band, not a budget. Pre-fills Mission 13.

  M8   Unit economics exists (price, cost to serve, margin). ADD:
       NEW calculated: "How many customers to break even?"
         fixed monthly costs (from the Cost Structure block)
         ÷ gross profit per customer
       Render as one sentence: "You need 84 customers a month to cover your
       costs." NOTHING TYPED.
       NEW calculated: MONEY MODEL SUMMARY strip —
         price · cost to serve · margin % · fixed costs · break-even
       The whole financial shape of the business on one line.

  M11  NEW calculated: "What did one result cost you?" = budget ÷ results,
       from the questions already in this mission. This is the founder's
       first cost-per-customer number and it costs them nothing to get.
       NEW calculated: "What would 100 cost at this rate?" shown beside the
       Mission 8 margin:
         "100 customers would cost 42,000. At your margin they'd be worth
          61,000."
       A channel's viability becomes obvious without one extra question.

  M12  NEW single-select PRICE LADDER, built from their own price point —
       three rungs below and three above:
         "At [price] — most said yes / some hesitated / most said no"
       The cheapest price research that exists, and founders usually already
       know the answer.
       NEW calculated: PAYBACK PERIOD = Mission 11 cost per customer ÷
       Mission 8 gross profit per customer, in months. Under 12 months is
       healthy — say so in one line.

  M13  NEW number + currency: "What's your budget to build it?" with
       "NOTHING — MY OWN TIME ONLY" as the FIRST option. Pre-filled from M5.
       NEW table: "What have you spent so far?" — line, amount, currency,
       RUNNING TOTAL SHOWN. Founders underestimate this by about half,
       because it arrives 200 at a time.

  M14  NEW number + currency: "Revenue so far", with "NO REVENUE YET" as the
       first option, carrying no penalty.
       NEW number: "How many people have actually paid you, ever?" — separate
       from sign-ups and usually far smaller.
       NEW calculated: average per customer = revenue ÷ paying customers,
       shown against the Mission 8 price:
         "You planned 200 per customer. You're averaging 140."
       That gap is one of the most useful things a founder can see, and
       neither number was typed.

  M15  NEW number + currency: "Cash available" — "Nothing" allowed.
       NEW number + currency: "What do you spend a month?" pre-filled from
       M13's spending log.
       NEW calculated: RUNWAY = cash ÷ monthly spend, in months.
         "At this rate you have 4 months."
       Every incubator asks this, and a founder who has never worked it out
       finds out in the room.
       NEW table + calculated: USE OF FUNDS, shown only if they are asking for
       money — category, amount, % of the ask, PERCENTAGES TOTALLED LIVE and
       flagged when they do not reach 100. Categories: Product · Marketing ·
       Team · Operations · Legal and licensing · Buffer.
       Add "I know my runway" to the readiness checklist.

FOUR RULES SO THIS DOES NOT BECOME THE THING THAT STOPS PEOPLE

1. "NOT SURE YET", "NOTHING" AND "NO REVENUE YET" ARE ALWAYS AVAILABLE AND
   NEVER PENALISED. Most founders reaching Mission 14 are pre-revenue. That is
   normal. A rubric that marks it down teaches founders to invent numbers,
   which is worse than having none.

2. NO MONEY QUESTION IS EVER REQUIRED TO PASS A MISSION. They enrich the
   assessment; they are not a gate.

3. ESTIMATE OR ACTUAL — a toggle on every number, stored with the value. An
   estimated runway and a real one are different things and the founder should
   be able to say which they meant. Show the label wherever the number appears,
   including in the exported PDF.

4. CURRENCY IS SET ONCE on the founder profile — EGP, SAR, AED, USD — and
   every field inherits it. Never ask twice. Format with the locale's own
   separators and put the symbol on the correct side for Arabic.

CONNECT TO THE EXISTING TOOLKIT — DO NOT REBUILD IT

The platform already has unit-economics, burn-and-runway and P&L calculators.
Mission 8's numbers open in the unit-economics calculator PRE-FILLED, and
Mission 15's open in burn and runway PRE-FILLED. One place to enter, two
places to use, and edits flow both ways.

THE FINANCIAL SUMMARY

Add a one-page financial summary to the Mission 15 export and to the founder's
dashboard:
  price · margin · break-even customers · cost per customer · payback period ·
  revenue to date · paying customers · monthly spend · runway
Every figure labelled estimate or actual, every figure traced to the mission
it came from. THIS IS THE PAGE AN INCUBATOR ASKS FOR, and by Mission 15 the
founder will have built it without ever seeing a spreadsheet.

One note, not a change

Mission 5 is named Solution Selection and its description says value/impact matrix, so I've built it exactly that way and left both untouched, as you asked.

Worth knowing for later: value and impact measure nearly the same thing, so ideas tend to land along a diagonal and the grid discriminates less than it could. A Value × Effort grid separates them into four genuinely different piles — do now, plan it, quick win, drop it. That's a one-line change to the second axis whenever you want it, and it changes no name.
