# 6. Proposed Approach and Engagement Structure
*(RFP §3.1 item 6 — 3 pages)*

## The problem we are engaged to solve

Innovators rarely fail to raise because their innovation is weak. They fail because the financial
case behind it cannot survive an hour of informed questioning: the model breaks under a scenario the
funder will certainly test, the cap table has locked in a structure that prices out the next round,
the runway assumption rests on a collection cycle nobody has verified, or the ask is for the wrong
instrument entirely.

Our work is to make the financial case true and defensible before it is tested by someone whose
decision the innovator cannot influence.

## Our operating principle

We build the analysis to institutional standard — discounted cash flow, scenario architecture, Monte
Carlo simulation where uncertainty is material — and then we hand the innovator something they can
operate themselves. An innovator who cannot answer a question about their own model has not been
helped; they have been given a document. Every engagement ends with the innovator able to run their
own numbers.

## What we deliver, by barrier

**The innovator does not know if the plan is financeable.** *Investment Readiness Diagnostic* — rapid
assessment across seven dimensions: financial record quality, model integrity, unit economics, capital
structure, governance readiness, evidence base and funder fit. Output is a scored readiness map and a
ranked list of what must be fixed before any approach to capital, sequenced by what blocks the round
soonest.

**The model will not survive due diligence.** *Financial Model and Scenario Build* — a driver-based
model covering revenue build, cost structure, headcount, working capital, grant and contract timing
and cash runway; scenario architecture across base, downside and stress, with Monte Carlo simulation
on the drivers carrying real uncertainty, expressed as a P10/P50/P90 range rather than a single
misleading number. Documented assumptions, change log, and a dashboard the innovator runs.

**The innovator does not know what to raise, from whom, or in what form.** *Capital Strategy and
Structuring* — funding requirement and drawdown profile; the right mix across grant, concessional and
commercial debt, equity, revenue-based instruments and blended structures; dilution and control
consequences modelled to the next two rounds; funder mapping by thesis, stage and cheque size; and an
ask that matches the instrument to the use of funds.

**The cap table is a problem the innovator cannot yet see.** *Cap Table Design and Repair* — current
structure, dilution across future rounds, option pool sizing, incentive alignment, and identification
of terms that will block or price down the next raise. Where damage is done, we model the realistic
repair paths and what each costs.

**The funder is coming and the innovator is not ready.** *Due Diligence Preparation and Data Room* —
an institutional-standard data room (model and historicals, corporate and legal documents, commercial
contracts, growth metrics, cap table, coordinated investment narrative) with the gap list closed
before the funder sees it. We run a mock diligence, questioning the innovator the way the funder will,
and fix what breaks.

**Valuation framing.** We provide a defensible range with methodology for negotiation — discounted
cash flow, comparables, precedent transactions. We do not issue formal or regulated valuation
opinions, and we tell innovators that plainly so they do not mistake a negotiating range for a
certified valuation.

## How this fits GCC's operating model

Engagements are commissioned individually and quoted as a fixed package with an hour ceiling before
they start — no open-ended mandates. GCC receives a scoping note at the front, a close-out note at
the end, and a monthly invoice itemising hours used against ceiling by innovator and grade, with
exception reporting the moment an engagement looks likely to over-run or under-deliver.

Every close-out note carries a "what this tells us" section. Where we see the same barrier across
several innovators we flag it as a portfolio pattern worth addressing once rather than five times —
which may mean recommending a Category 5 cohort session instead of five separate engagements.

**A conflicts rule that matters here:** we advise both companies raising capital and investors
conducting diligence. We will not act on both sides of the same transaction, and we disclose any
existing relationship with a funder an innovator is approaching before accepting the engagement.

## How we track whether it worked

We report against outcomes, not activity. For every Category 1 engagement we record the decision the
innovator took, and at 3 and 6 months post-close whether it held:

| Measured | How |
|---|---|
| Decision made within the engagement window | Close-out note, confirmed by innovator |
| Model operated by the innovator unaided 90 days later | Follow-up check |
| Capital raised, instrument, terms — where a raise followed | Innovator-confirmed, at 6 months |
| Diligence outcome where preparation was the objective | Innovator-confirmed |
| Runway extension achieved | Model-verified against baseline |

We are careful about attribution. A raise that closes after our engagement is not automatically our
result, and we will not claim it as one. We report what we contributed and let GCC and the innovator
judge the rest.

## Example 2 — Fifteen scenarios that changed what got built

**Client:** `[[Name or descriptor]]` · **Period:** `[[...]]` · **Reference:** `[[#]]`

**The problem.** A development was proceeding on a product mix set by convention rather than
analysis. The projected internal rate of return was acceptable but unremarkable, and nobody had
tested whether a different configuration of the same asset would perform materially better. The
decision window was closing — past a certain point the mix would be locked by construction.

**What we did.** We built fifteen full scenarios for the product mix, each modelled end to end
against price per square metre achievable by type, absorption capacity in the actual local market,
cost structure by typology, and the resulting return profile. Rather than presenting a
recommendation, we presented the frontier — showing the client exactly what each configuration cost
and returned, and where the supply-constrained segment sat.

**What changed.** The client shifted the mix toward the segment the analysis identified as
supply-constrained, capturing a materially higher price per square metre than the original
configuration would have achieved, and improving the projected internal rate of return
`[[from X% to Y% — insert or delete]]`. The decision was taken inside the window, before the mix was
locked. **Attribution:** the scenario analysis is directly attributable to us; the client made and
executed the decision.

## Example 3 — Timing an exit against the valuation cycle

**Client:** `[[Name or descriptor]]` · **Period:** `[[...]]` · **Reference:** `[[#]]`

**The problem.** An owner held a hospitality asset and was considering divestment, working from a
single indicative valuation and a single interested buyer. There was no analysis of where the asset
sat in its valuation cycle and no competitive tension in the process.

**What we did.** We analysed the valuation cycle for the asset class and market to identify the
window, built the financial case supporting a higher valuation than the indicative figure, and ran a
targeted outreach process to a mapped set of buyers whose investment thesis fit the asset — rather
than waiting on the one buyer already at the table.

**What changed.** The process produced competing offers above the initial indicative valuation, and
the exit was executed within the identified window. **Attribution:** the cycle analysis, the
valuation case and the buyer mapping were ours; the transaction was the client's.

## What these have in common

Three different asset classes, one method: build the analysis the decision actually requires, put the
real options in front of the decision-maker with their consequences quantified, and let them decide
with the downside tested. That is the same method we would bring to a GCC innovator deciding whether
to raise, what to raise, and from whom.

**Portfolio-level track record relevant to this category:** over 100 early-stage ventures advised
from idea stage to first round; more than 50 financial models built and independently reviewed
across software-as-a-service, e-commerce, financial technology, climate technology and real estate;
more than USD 10 million in capital raised or supported; independent due diligence delivered on
behalf of investors, which is the discipline that tells us precisely what an innovator's model will
be asked on the other side of the table.
