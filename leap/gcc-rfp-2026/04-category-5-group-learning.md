# 6. Proposed Approach and Engagement Structure
*(RFP §3.1 item 6 — 3 pages)*

## The problem we are engaged to solve

Earlier-stage innovators are usually stuck on the same handful of foundational things: what their
business model actually is, whether their unit economics work, what a funder will ask, what a
realistic operating plan looks like at their stage. Answering those one innovator at a time is slow
and expensive. Answering them badly — a lecture, a slide deck, a recorded webinar nobody finishes —
is worse, because it produces the appearance of support without changing anything.

We design group learning that ends with each innovator holding something they built during the
session and can use the next day.

## Why we are credible here

We have advised over 100 early-stage ventures from idea stage to first round. The curriculum is not
theoretical: it is the set of questions we have answered repeatedly, one founder at a time, distilled
into the form that works for a group. The RFP asks for a balance of facilitation and subject-matter
expertise — our facilitators are the practitioners who do the 1:1 work in Categories 1, 2 and 4.

## Design principles

**Work, not watch.** Every session is built around a tool the innovator completes with their own
numbers, live, with facilitator support. If a session could have been a recording, we designed it
wrong.

**Cohort-matched.** We group by stage and shared barrier, not convenience — a pre-revenue innovator
and one negotiating a second round need different sessions.

**Small enough to be real.** 8–15 innovators per cohort. Above that participants stop speaking and
the peer exchange, usually the most valuable part, does not happen.

**Built for real conditions.** Low-bandwidth-tolerant platforms, 90-minute cap, scheduled across time
zones, recorded, with written summaries and tools distributed in advance.

**Arabic and English.** Cohorts delivered natively in either. `[[Add French or Spanish only with a
named associate who facilitates in them.]]`

## What we deliver

**Virtual group-learning sessions.** 90-minute working sessions on foundational concepts drawn from
Categories 1–4. Standard modules we can deliver from existing material:

| Module | Drawn from | What each innovator leaves with |
|---|---|---|
| What your business model actually is | Cat 2 | A completed model canvas with their own figures |
| Unit economics from first principles | Cat 2 | Their own cost-per-unit and break-even calculation |
| Reading your own financial model | Cat 1 | Their assumptions mapped, with the three that matter identified |
| Cash, runway and the decisions they force | Cat 1 | A 13-week cash view of their own position |
| What a funder will actually ask you | Cat 1 | Their gap list against a diligence checklist |
| Choosing your funding instrument | Cat 1 | A funding pathway matched to their use of funds |
| Scaling pathways and what breaks first | Cat 2 | Their own ranked barrier register |
| Entering a new market: what to check before you commit | Cat 4 | A market-readiness checklist for their target |

`[[Confirm which of these you already have material for and which need building — the internal plan
carries the build estimate.]]`

**Cohort modules.** Four linked sessions over 6–8 weeks with a single through-line, structured
between-session work, and office hours. This is where behaviour actually changes: the innovator
applies each session to their own organisation before the next one.

**Peer knowledge exchange.** Facilitated convenings — virtual, or in person where GCC wishes —
structured around a shared problem, with prepared prompts and a written synthesis afterwards. We
facilitate rather than present; the content comes from the room.

**Resource development.** Practical tools for earlier-stage innovators: worked templates, model
skeletons, checklists and short written guides, in Arabic and English, designed to be used unaided.
Everything we build for GCC is GCC's to reuse across the portfolio.

## How this fits GCC's operating model

- Group support is for innovators not receiving 1:1 support, per the FAQ. We will not enrol an
  innovator we are separately advising 1:1 without GCC's explicit direction.
- Cohort design starts from GCC's read of portfolio need. We propose the curriculum; GCC approves it.
- Cross-provider coordination is GCC's to run; we make our schedule, materials and cohort lists
  available to support it.
- Our 1:1 practice feeds the curriculum. Where we see a barrier recurring across several innovators,
  we propose a session rather than five separate engagements — better value to GCC, even though it is
  the smaller commission for us.

## How we track whether it worked

Attendance is not an outcome. We measure:

| Measured | Target | How |
|---|---|---|
| Attendance against enrolment | ≥ 80% | Register |
| Completion of the in-session tool | ≥ 75% of participants | Facilitator check, artefacts collected |
| Participant rating of usefulness (1–5) | ≥ 4.2 | Post-session survey |
| "I will change something as a result" — with the change named | ≥ 70% | Post-session survey |
| Change actually made | ≥ 50% | 60-day follow-up on a sample |
| Cohort completion (multi-session) | ≥ 75% attend 3 of 4 | Register |

We report the 60-day follow-up honestly, including when it is disappointing. A session that rates
well and changes nothing is a failure we would rather diagnose than repeat.
