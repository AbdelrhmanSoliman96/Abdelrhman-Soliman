# 6. Proposed Approach and Engagement Structure
*(RFP §3.1 item 6 — 3 pages)*

## What we claim, and what we do not

This RFP says local expertise is an asset in this category, and we agree — which is why we scope this
application tightly rather than claiming global coverage.

**We apply for this category in Egypt, and in Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait and
Qatar.** These are markets where we hold a physical presence or active engagements, where we know how
commercial and regulatory decisions are really made, and where we work in Arabic. Egypt is our head
office and our deepest market.

**We do not claim, and will not accept engagements requiring:** health-product regulatory registration
and approval work — for example medical device or pharmaceutical dossiers with the Egyptian Drug
Authority or the Saudi Food and Drug Authority — unless delivered with a named specialist
subcontractor proposed to GCC in advance. We also do not claim market navigation outside MENA. We
would rather GCC route those needs to a provider who does them properly.

Stating this plainly is deliberate. An innovator who discovers mid-engagement that their adviser was
stretching is worse off than one matched correctly at the start.

## Our operating principle

Market entry fails on the things that are not written down: which approvals are actually enforced
versus nominally required, how long a process really takes against its stated timeline, which
partner structures are workable for a foreign or non-profit entity, and who has to be persuaded
before a procurement decision is possible. We work from the documented framework *and* from how the
market actually behaves, and we are explicit about which is which.

## How we scope an engagement

Same rhythm as our other categories — triage within 5 business days, one-page scoping note within 3
days, evidence intake, delivery, decision session, close-out note to GCC — with one addition specific
to this category: **at triage we confirm we are the right provider for that specific market.** If we
are not, we say so immediately rather than accepting the commission.

## What we deliver, by barrier

**"We do not know what it takes to operate here."**
*Market and Regulatory Landscape Assessment.* For one named market: the regulatory and licensing
framework applicable to the innovation; which authorities have jurisdiction and what each actually
requires; realistic timelines and costs against stated ones; the legal forms available to a foreign
or non-profit entity and their consequences; and the constraints that would stop the model as
currently designed. Output is an assessment with a compliance checklist and a clear statement of what
is verified against primary source versus what is practitioner knowledge.

**"We do not know how to enter, or with whom."**
*Entry and Partnership Pathway Design.* Entry-mode options — direct registration, subsidiary, local
partnership, distribution agreement, hosting under an established local entity — compared on cost,
speed, control, regulatory exposure and reversibility. Where partnership is the route, we define the
partner profile, screen a shortlist, make the introduction, and support the commercial framework
through to heads of terms. Our network across five markets is the substance of this offer.

**"We need to be adopted by the public system and do not know how."**
*Government Engagement and Procurement Pathway Support.* How the relevant ministry or public agency
actually procures; whether the innovation fits an existing budget line or needs a new one; who the
decision-makers and technical gatekeepers are; what evidence and accreditation they will require; and
a sequenced engagement plan with the materials to support it. We prepare the innovator for the
meeting; we do not lobby, and we do not represent the innovator to government without written
authority and full disclosure to GCC.

**"We need this in Arabic."**
*Arabic-language delivery.* All of the above delivered natively in Arabic — working sessions,
documents, and support in meetings with local authorities and partners. Appendix A names Arabic
explicitly among GCC's priority languages. For an Arabic-speaking innovator, working through their
own language on a regulatory question is the difference between understanding a framework and being
told about one.

## Local delivery and associates

Where an engagement needs presence or relationships we do not hold directly, we engage a named local
associate under our contract, proposed to GCC before the engagement starts, with LEAP remaining fully
responsible for quality and delivery. We will not accept a Category 4 engagement in a market where we
can offer neither direct standing nor a named associate.

## Integrity commitments

We comply with anti-bribery, anti-corruption and sanctions law without exception. We do not make or
facilitate facilitation payments. If an engagement cannot progress without one, we stop and report to
GCC. Given this category involves government engagement, we state that up front rather than in a
schedule.

## How we track whether it worked

| Measured | How |
|---|---|
| Regulatory pathway documented and validated against primary source | Close-out note, with sources cited |
| Entry decision taken within the engagement window | Innovator-confirmed |
| Partner introductions made, meetings held, agreements reached | Tracked and reported per engagement |
| Registration, licence or approval progressed | Innovator-confirmed at 3 and 6 months |
| Procurement or adoption milestone reached | Innovator-confirmed at 6 months |

Regulatory timelines are outside anyone's control. We report what we advanced and what remains
pending, and we do not claim an approval as our outcome.
