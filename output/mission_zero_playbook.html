What changed in this version

Cairo is dropped — one launch, on the Alexandria stage, which is the bigger event and the one with the youth. The scope is all 15 missions in one month, not 8 in six weeks. We run and manage the whole thing, which is now the core of what Techne is being offered. Everything below is rebuilt around those three decisions.

01 / THE READWhat Techne is, in four parts

  • Techne Summit Alexandria — 3–5 October 2026. 60,000+ attendees last edition, 1,500 startups, 250 investors, 400 speakers, under the auspices of MCIT, ITIDA, DETGD and FEDCOC. This is the launch stage.
  • Techne Drifts — a year-round, one-day roadshow across 14 Egyptian cities, aimed at university students and youth entrepreneurs. This is the distribution network, and the natural home for the Demo Day in November.
  • Compete — their competition track: 50+ competitions to date, and in 2026 the Startup World Cup regional tournament in Alexandria on 4 October, whose winner goes to San Francisco for a USD 1,000,000 prize.
  • Techne Summit Cairo — 26–27 September, the corporate end. Not used this year.
The positioning that makes this work

Do not describe this as a startup competition. Techne's own tournament that week carries a USD 1,000,000 headline, and Techne already owns the national youth roadshow competition. Mission Zero is a qualification challenge for people who do not have a startup yet — no company, no funding, no revenue, written into the eligibility rules — so it cannot draw a single entrant away from Compete, and it feeds their track next year.

02 / THE CASEWhy this is worth it for both sides

The offer to Techne is not sponsorship money. It is a finished programme they neither build nor staff nor pay for, that keeps their brand working for a month after their doors close.

What Techne gets

  • A month of post-event life. Every summit's value dies on the last day. This one keeps a Techne-branded programme running until 4 November, with a Demo Day in late November — three months of visibility from a three-day event.
  • Programming at zero cost and zero load. We fund the EGP 100,000, produce it, staff it, judge it and handle the participants.
  • Stage content for Alexandria that is participatory rather than another panel — a room where everyone finishes a mission on their phone.
  • A qualified feeder into Compete 2027 — our top ten arrive having completed fifteen missions of documented work, not a cold application form.
  • A scoring engine for Techne Drifts, replacing ad-hoc screening across 14 cities with one published rubric.
  • Governorate-level data on youth entrepreneurship — the outcome reporting a summit held under MCIT and ITIDA auspices actually needs.
  • Free StartPad licences for all Drifts participants for twelve months.

What GrowthLabs gets

  • Distribution we cannot buy. 60,000 attendees plus a national university network, at the cost of a booth.
  • The first end-to-end cohort. Nobody has yet run a group of founders through all fifteen missions. This produces that dataset — where they stall, which missions take longest, what completion actually costs.
  • Proof the evaluator works at scale, which is the single hardest thing to demonstrate when selling CatalystOS to a government or a university.
  • A reference case with Techne's name on it — worth more in an ITIDA or university meeting than any deck.
  • Pipeline. Finishers flow into Startup Gate and into GrowthLabs programmes; the top three take incubation seats.
  • Content for a year — 50+ completed founder journeys, real benchmarks, real testimonials.

The one real problem with fifteen missions in thirty days — and the fix

Missions 9, 13 and 14 ask a founder to build a prototype, ship an MVP and test product-market fit. Left undefined, a student reads that as "build an app", and in thirty days you get either fabricated answers or twelve finishers.

The fix is to define "done" narrowly and publish it on day one. None of the definitions below weaken the mission — they are what a lean founder should be doing at this stage anyway, and they are what makes the month honest rather than optimistic.

MissionAccepted as completeExplicitly not required
9 · Build PrototypeA clickable prototype (Figma, or any no-code builder), a paper prototype photographed, or a scripted walkthrough — shown to at least 5 people Any written code
13 · Build MVPA manual MVP: a landing page with real sign-ups, a concierge service delivered by hand, a WhatsApp or form-based service — with at least 10 real users or sign-upsA deployed product, an app store listing, payments
14 · Product-Market Fit TestA demand-signal test on ≥20 target users: the Sean Ellis question, a pre-order or waitlist conversion, or a paid-intent test A real PMF score — nobody has product-market fit in four weeks, and the mission should say so rather than ask for a self-rating out of ten
15 · Go to MarketA 90-day plan with one named channel, a budget and a target — plus one channel actually tested during the monthExecuted campaigns, spend

This also fixes something the mission audit already flagged in the product: Mission 14 currently asks founders to self-rate product-market fit from one to ten, which is exactly the unfalsifiable answer the evidence rubric exists to catch. With money attached it would be worse. Replacing it with a demand-signal test for the competition is the right call regardless of Techne.

06 / JUDGINGBuilt assuming people will try to game it

The moment cash is attached to an AI-scored submission, some entrants will write for the evaluator instead of talking to customers. Six defences, all announced before entries open:

  1. The AI score filters, it never decides. It takes the top ~25% through to human review. Say this publicly so nobody believes they are negotiating with a machine.
  2. Evidence artefacts, not prose. Every interview claim carries a log: first name, city, date, channel, consent checkbox. No log, no marks on that question.
  3. Verification calls to a random 10% of finalists' logged interviewees. The announcement does more work than the calls.
  4. Duplicate and template detection — one popular tutorial produces forty identical problem statements.
  5. A blind human panel scores the shortlist with names and universities removed.
  6. The live pitch decides. Three minutes, four minutes of questions. Very little survives an unscripted question from a judge who has read the submission.

The published rubric

PillarWeightWhat earns the marks
Evidence30%Real conversations with real people, logged — and how much the answers changed the founder's mind.

11 / RISKS

RiskHandling
Techne does not answer in time. Twenty-four days out, their team is consumed by their own event.Send today, phone Friday, treat Sunday 13 Sept as go/no-go. Fallback ladder: no stage slot → run the workshop at the booth on the hour; no booth → buy passes and run it as an official side event, which Techne offers; nothing this year → launch at the Drifts finale and sign a 2027 track now.
Fifteen missions is too much and the field collapses. The main risk in this version.Published definitions of done, weekly clinics, non-eliminating checkpoints. Set the tripwire now: if fewer than 120 are through week 2 by 18 Oct, publicly reduce the requirement to missions 1–12 — early and announced, never at judging time.
Entrants write for the AI.All six defences in section 06, announced before entries open.
"They will steal my idea." A real blocker with young Egyptian founders. Put "You own your idea. Always." on the landing page, in the rules and on the stage. No IP assignment, no right of first refusal — if anyone proposes adding one, the clause is worth less than the competition.
Personal data of a thousand young people.Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law (151 of 2020) applies: explicit consent at registration, stated retention, a named data owner, and no sharing of entrant contact details with sponsors.
Prize payment.Bank transfer against national ID and a signed receipt. Have an accountant confirm the tax treatment of prize money to individuals before announcing.
A judge knows a finalist.Blind shortlist scoring, declared conflicts, 72-hour appeal window.

12 / NAMEMission Zero

The missions are the product, so the name comes from them. Mission Zero — المهمة صفر — is the mission before the missions: the decision to start. It works in both languages, it avoids the word "competition", and it says the entry point is zero, which is exactly the audience.
