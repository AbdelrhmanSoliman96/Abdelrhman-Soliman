# Post 3: The same fund can be 1.5x and 1.2x (MOIC vs TVPI)

Pillar: investor operations. Publish Wed 18 Nov (LinkedIn carousel + X thread), Thu 19 Nov (reel).
All numbers are an illustrative fund and are labelled as such on every visual. No Angelsta data.

## Check of the arithmetic

- LPs paid in: $10M. Invested in companies: $8M. Fees and expenses: $2M. Residual value of holdings: $12M. No distributions. No carry accrued.
- MOIC on investments = $12M ÷ $8M = **1.5x**
- TVPI = ($0 distributions + $12M residual) ÷ $10M paid-in = **1.2x**
- DPI = $0 ÷ $10M = **0.0x**. RVPI = $12M ÷ $10M = **1.2x**. TVPI = DPI + RVPI = 1.2x.

## LinkedIn post (attach `linkedin-carousel.pdf`)

**HOOK LINE**
The same fund can be 1.5x and 1.2x at the same time. Both numbers are correct.

**BODY**
The difference is the denominator.

Take an illustrative fund. Limited partners paid in $10M. $8M went into companies and $2M went to fees and expenses. The companies are now worth $12M.

MOIC on the investments is $12M ÷ $8M, which is 1.5x. It tells you how the deals are performing.

TVPI divides the same $12M by the $10M that was paid in. That is 1.2x, before carry. It tells you what the investor's position is worth against everything they put in, fees included.

Nothing has been distributed yet, so DPI is 0.0x and all of TVPI is RVPI. TVPI is always DPI plus RVPI.

**CLOSE**
When someone quotes you a multiple, which denominator are they using?
